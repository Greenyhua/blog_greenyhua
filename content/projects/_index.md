<h3>1. Research on Social Familiarity and Self in the Uncanny Valley: A Multidimensional Exploration (Master Thesis)</h3>

*Author & Researcher \| Advisor: Prof. Guomei Zhou*  
*Duration: December 2024 – Present*

This project investigates the **uncanny valley effect** through the lens of social cognition and self-concept, focusing on emotional, cognitive, and social mechanisms.  
Key contributions:  
- Designed and implemented experiments manipulating variables such as **human-likeness**, **familiarity**, and **self-referential stimuli** to test major uncanny valley hypotheses.  
- Developed a **multidimensional framework of social familiarity** and analyzed its interactions with emotional and social-cognitive processes using **data visualization** and **modeling techniques**.  
- Proposed future expansions, including:  
  - The application of **machine learning models** to simulate uncanny valley-related emotional responses.  
  - Exploring real-time **human-AI interactions** using Virtual Reality technologies.  
</div>

<h3>2. The Role of Self-Concept in Shaping the Uncanny Valley: A Meta-Analytic Approach</h3>

*Researcher \| Advisor: Prof. Guomei Zhou*  
*Duration: March 2025 – Present*

This project explores how **self-concept perception** influences the depth and position of the uncanny valley effect through a **systematic meta-analysis**.  
Key contributions:  
- Conducted a **comprehensive literature review** and selected studies based on defined criteria.  
- Extracted and coded data for **meta-analytic statistical analysis**.  
- Interpreted results and contributed to the **development of theoretical frameworks** and report preparation.  
</div>

<h3>3. Development and Validation of the Scale of Human Threat Perception and Defensive Mechanisms Toward Robots</h3>

*Researcher \| Advisor: Prof. Guomei Zhou*  
*Duration: April 2025 – Present*

This project focuses on developing and validating a scale to measure human perceptions of threat and defensive responses toward robots, emphasizing emotional, cognitive, and behavioral dimensions.  
Key contributions:  
- Conducted an extensive **literature review** to refine the theoretical framework and identify key constructs of the scale.  
- Analyzed the scale’s **reliability** and **validity** using **exploratory factor analysis (EFA)** and **confirmatory factor analysis (CFA)**.  
</div>

<div class="card" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
<h3>4. Customer Subscription Prediction for Bank Products Based on the LightGBM Model</h3>

*Author & Researcher \| Advisor: Associate Prof. Ying Lin*  
*Duration: October 2024 – January 2025*

This project focused on developing a **machine learning model** to predict customer subscriptions for banking products, leveraging advanced optimization techniques.  
Key contributions:  
- Preprocessed data, including **feature encoding**, **standardization**, and **dataset splitting**.  
- Optimized machine learning models (**Decision Tree**, **Random Forest**, **XGBoost**, **LightGBM**) using **Optuna** for hyperparameter tuning.  
- Visualized results and contributed to the final report, highlighting key findings and actionable insights.  
</div>
